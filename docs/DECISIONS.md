# Decisions

Last verified: 2026-09-06 | 2026-09-06

Decisions by Paweł, dated. Later entries win.

- 2026-09-06 **ONE is the fifth collection of onenft.click**: 10,000 coins per series, series without end, 50 Master Coins (1/1) per series, each other coin procedural. Art and money are two independent axes: a Master Coin can carry 10, 25 or 50 USDC and a plain coin can carry 50.
- 2026-09-06 **Model A**: each coin holds shares of an ERC-4626 USDC vault on Base. Burn returns backing plus earned yield minus a 10 percent fee on the yield. The contract has no admin over the pool: no pause on redeem, no upgrade, no key to the funds. Paweł chose to proceed knowing the MiCA and tax questions; there was no lawyer.
- 2026-09-06 **Price equals backing**: 10, 25 or 50 USDC, nothing on top. The author's revenue is the 10 percent of yield and the founder coins.
- 2026-09-06 **Randomness is Chainlink VRF v2.5** on Base. The art slot comes from a lazy Fisher-Yates over the series' 10,000 slots, so exactly 50 masters per series and nobody, the author included, can steer them.
- 2026-09-06 **Founder coins**: 50 reserved slots per series. The author mints them one at a time, paying the backing from the author wallet or from the fees the contract has collected. Their art comes from the same urn. Trait `Origin: Founder`.
- 2026-09-06 **Yield ring**: the dynamic layer reads lifetime yield over backing (basis points). Claims do not reset it; transfers do not reset it. Levels at 0.01, 1, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 35, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 300 and 500 percent.
- 2026-09-06 **Renderer rules**: integers only, no trigonometry, symmetry through `rotate()`; the seed's top 32 bits are engraved as hex, the low 32 bits as ticks on the rim. Backing class is a small number on the rim, never a material.
- 2026-09-06 **Pixel art, like every other collection of onenft.click** (Paweł, after seeing a vector draft). 64 by 64 grid, coin radius 23, one path per colour in the SVG (about 12 KB a coin). The vector renderer was thrown away the same day.
- 2026-09-06 **Master Coins are 25 compositions in two palettes each**, not fourteen in three or four: Paweł asked for more variety between recipes. Names kept from the first list where they fit.
- 2026-09-06 **Site before contracts.** The site ran in preview mode from a simulated series until the contract existed. Superseded the same day by the entry below: the preview series is gone and every page reads the chain.
- 2026-09-06 **Founder coins are free for the author** (Paweł: "wchodzę portfelem, mintuję 50 x 50 USDC, tyle"). The author mints up to 50 a series from the author wallet at no cost, class 50, art from the same urn as everyone. Their backing is not paid at mint: the contract fills it from the author's 10 percent of yield, oldest founder coin first, until each holds 50 USDC. Until then the coin shows its funded amount, earns yield on what is funded, and a burn returns what is funded. Nobody else's money ever backs a founder coin. Replaces the 2026-09-06 founder entry above.
- 2026-09-06 **No per-wallet limit** (Paweł: "ktoś, kto chce wydać 5000 dolarów, ma móc"). The only cap is the series itself. A mint transaction takes up to 10 coins at once (one VRF request, 10 random words; the VRF callback cap on Base is 2,500,000 gas), so 5,000 USDC of 50 USDC coins is ten transactions. The reason for a batch cap is gas, not policy.
- 2026-09-06 **Vault: Spark USDC Vault on Morpho, Base** (`0x7BfA7C4f149E7415b73bdeDfe609237e29CBF34A`, listed, about 306M USD, net APY 3.8% on 2026-09-06). Chosen for size and curator over the small APY edge of Gauntlet USDC Prime. The vault address is immutable in the token; a change means a new contract. Chainlink VRF v2.5 on Base: coordinator `0xd5D517aBE5cF79B7e95eC98dB0f0277788aFF634`, 2 gwei lane, native payment, max callback 2,500,000 gas (so batches are 10, not 20).
- 2026-09-06 **Two rules added after the Cato audit of OneCoin.** (a) A sealed coin cannot be redeemed, and the VRF callback draws one urn slot per minted id whether or not the coin still exists: without this a holder could watch Chainlink's answer in the mempool and burn chosen sealed coins to push later coins of the batch onto a Master Coin slot. (b) The minter pays the randomness fee in ETH at mint, forwarded to the VRF subscription (a floor set at deploy, in the order of 0.00005 ETH per transaction), and a coin can be redeemed no sooner than 30 days after its mint; claims of yield are allowed at any time. Without this, mint, reveal and redeem cost nothing, so a bot could churn tickets, keep the masters, burn the rest and drain the author's VRF subscription. The USDC price stays equal to the backing.
- 2026-09-06 **Founder pacing.** Founder coin k of a series can be minted only when the coin being minted is at least the 200 times k-th coin of that series (so the fiftieth can be the series' last coin), so the author's free mints are spread across the series and cannot wait for a moment when the urn holds many masters. The odds of the urn (`mastersLeft` over `urnLeft`) are public to everyone; that is fine, since every public minter sees the same numbers and pays the same price.
- 2026-09-06 **VRF coordinator migration.** The consumer base carries `setCoordinator`, callable only by the current coordinator, as Chainlink's migration path expects. A second contract briefly let the owner call it too (Chainlink's own base does); the third audit round showed that this lets the author point the token at a coordinator of their own and choose every Master Coin, which breaks the public claim that nobody can steer the urn. The owner path is gone again; a coordinator that never answers is covered by the 180 day sealed escape instead. The second mainnet contract, `0x4Bd8…4a72`, carries that path and is retired for it.
- 2026-09-06 **Vault limits and ownership, after the audit.** A mint reverts when the vault will not take the deposit; a claim pays what the vault can release now; a redeem reverts, coin intact, when the vault cannot release the full amount, so the holder tries again later. The owner of the contract is the author for good: both `renounceOwnership` and `transferOwnership` revert. The renderer is set only by the author and only plain contracts are ever set (no proxies); the contract cannot prove that, the deploy record does.
- 2026-09-06 **The site reads the chain, and only the chain.** `src/preview.ts` keeps two functions (the sealed stand-in and a coin from any seed, for the Master Coin gallery and the yield page); the simulated series and `PREVIEW_SUPPLY` are gone. With no `CONTRACT_ADDRESS` the site is a plain renderer showing the empty state, and it never invents a count: a chain that never answered gives `null` in the JSON and a question mark on the page, never a zero.
- 2026-09-06 **The page wears the newest coin that has its art.** A sealed coin is grey on purpose, and every mint puts one at the head, so wearing it would drain the whole site of colour until Chainlink answers. `/newest.svg` and the hero still show the newest coin, sealed or not.
- 2026-09-06 **`/api/state.totalSupply` is the current series' mint count, against `maxSupply` 10,000.** The hub renders "N of maxSupply" for every collection, and a global counter would read past 10,000 in series II. The global figure is `mintedEver`, the coins that still exist are `live`, and `poolLeft` is the Master Coin slots left in the urn, which is what the hub calls the 1/1 pool.
- 2026-09-06 **No autoclaim, one keeper job.** Chainlink VRF answers by itself, so nothing on the server signs a mint. `src/keeper.ts` only calls `retry(requestId)` for requests past `RETRY_BLOCKS`, and only when `DEPLOYER_KEY` is set. A retry can add a chance of a seed and never take one away.
- 2026-09-06 **The browser builds its own calldata.** The mint, claim and redeem flows use `eth_sendTransaction` with selectors from `src/contract.ts` and no ABI encoder in the page, the way Faces does it. `src/abi.test.ts` checks every signature against the compiled `OneCoin.json`, so a contract change breaks a test rather than a live page.
- 2026-09-06 **Every revert the reader can meet has a sentence.** `REVERTS` in `src/contract.ts` maps the selector of each error a minter or a holder can hit to plain words, and both browser flows match on it, so a full vault, an early founder coin, an illiquid vault, a coin still inside its thirty days or a sealed one read as English instead of four bytes of hex. `src/abi.test.ts` checks each of those errors exists in the compiled contract.
- 2026-09-06 **Sealed escape.** A coin sealed for 180 days can be redeemed after all. Chainlink's coordinator blocks migration and consumer removal while a request is pending, so a request the node never answers would otherwise lock the batch's money for good. The steering attack that the sealed-burn rule defends against needs an answer in flight; after half a year there is none, and a late answer draws one slot per id whatever happened to the coins. The fee on a partial claim is charged on the gain actually paid out, not on the whole gain, so no unit of yield pays the fee twice.
- 2026-09-06 **Two facts about the randomness fee, stated once.** The ETH a minter attaches goes straight into the VRF subscription the author owns; it pays for randomness and the author can close that subscription and take what is left only when no request is pending. The fee is fixed at deploy, so its bite against churn shrinks if gas on Base rises; `retry` is payable and open to anyone, so a subscription that runs dry can always be refilled by whoever wants a coin opened.
- 2026-09-06 **The site reads the founder band, it does not compute it.** `founderWindow(series)` gives k, the band's first and last coin and whether it is open, so the page never re-derives a rule the contract owns. One coin per band: the next band opens two hundred coins later, so a batch could not fit inside one.
- 2026-09-06 **The sealed escape is on the coin page.** A coin the VRF never answered for names the date its backing comes free, and Burn opens on that date; without saying so the page would show a coin that looks stuck for good.
- 2026-09-06 **Second mainnet contract, by Paweł after the first founder mint.** A series is 25,000 coins; four backing classes, 5, 10, 25 and 50 USDC; 100 founder coins a series, all mintable only inside the series' first 1,000 positions (so the author takes them at the start and has no moment to wait for), replacing the one-per-200 bands. Master Coins stay 50 a series (the renderer holds exactly 50 recipes), so the odds are 50 in 25,000. The first contract, `0xF597D7bD4467A501a7634dD53Be63E1c7261bcdB`, keeps its one founder coin as a relic and is retired.
- 2026-09-06 **The series' shape is read, not written down.** The site asks the contract for `SERIES_SIZE`, `MASTERS`, `FOUNDERS_PER_SERIES`, `MAX_BATCH`, `FOUNDER_WINDOW` and the backing classes in the same multicall as the counters, and every page takes them from `factsOf(chain)`. A deploy with 25,000 coins a series, a hundred founder coins or a fifth backing class changes the copy on its own. The constants left in `src/contract.ts` are named `DEFAULT_` and only serve a server with no contract.
- 2026-09-06 **Founder coins are a start window, not a pace.** `founderWindow(series)` gives how many are out, how many are left, the coin the window shuts at and whether it is open; the box says which, caps the count at what is left, and states plainly that anything unminted when the window closes is forfeited.
- 2026-09-06 **The risk is said plainly, in one voice, in five places.** `RISK` in `src/contract.ts` holds the author's words once; `/how#risk` prints them in full, and the mint box, the sidebar, the footer and both JSON answers quote or link them. A reader cannot reach the mint button without passing the sentence that says this can lose them money.
- 2026-09-06 **Coin images carry the contract in their URL.** `?c=<first 8 hex of the contract>` on every coin image the site emits, as Faces does. Ids restart at 1 with a new contract, so a browser or a CDN holding coin 1 from the last one would otherwise serve the wrong picture. The tag lets a revealed coin's image be held an hour rather than a minute; it is never immutable, because the yield ring grows with the coin's lifetime yield and a sealed coin changes the moment its seed lands.
